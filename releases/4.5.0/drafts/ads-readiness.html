DRAFT FOR REVIEW

Is your book ready for Amazon Ads?

Before paying for more attention, check whether the book’s positioning and listing give the intended reader a clear reason to consider it.

Advertising starts with a reader proposition

Begin with a practical question: who is this book for, and what reading experience does it offer? The answer should come from the book, rather than a list of search terms you hope will attract attention.

For fiction, consider the genre, central situation and emotional promise. For nonfiction, consider the reader’s problem, the book’s approach and what it actually covers. These are positioning questions, not evidence that the book is guaranteed to convert.

Imagine a quiet family novel introduced as a relentless action thriller. This is a hypothetical example. Advertising might bring interested thriller readers to the page, but the mismatch would still need attention. More traffic does not resolve an unclear or inaccurate proposition.

Review the listing as a new reader

Look at the cover at thumbnail size, the title and format, the opening description and the visible book details. Do they describe the same kind of book? Can someone unfamiliar with the author understand the proposition without reading a long introduction?

Check the mobile page as well. Small type inside an A+ graphic or a confusing description can make the presentation harder to use. The aim is to identify specific issues, not declare the whole listing “bad” from a quick glance.

The My Dear Grandfather listing case study illustrates a documented description and research diagnosis. Its later ranking observations remain qualified. It is not an advertising case study or proof of advertising profitability.

Separate platform eligibility from commercial readiness

Amazon’s official Sponsored Products page describes the format as cost-per-click advertising and explains keyword and product targeting options, bids and budgets. It lists KDP authors among the groups the service can support. Eligibility and available options must still be checked for the relevant account, book and marketplace.

Platform eligibility does not establish that the book is ready to advertise profitably. A book can be eligible while its proposition, listing or commercial assumptions need more work.

A review count, star rating or budget is not a universal guarantee of readiness. Do not turn a rule of thumb into a platform requirement. Check current official eligibility and policy information inside the relevant advertising account before launch.

Decide what a test should help you learn

Agree the purpose of the campaign. You may be testing relevant targeting, learning which searches attract attention, or reviewing how advertising fits a wider launch plan. A clear purpose makes the initial structure and subsequent decisions easier to assess.

Set a budget the client understands and authorises. Keep advertising spend separate from the professional service fee unless the written agreement says otherwise. Decide who will monitor the account, when it will be reviewed and what information will be available.

Setup alone is not ongoing management. A campaign left without an agreed review owner should not be described as a complete optimisation service.

Use the framework to choose the next action

In the GRANT Framework, advertising primarily supports N: Navigate visibility channels. Market fit, positioning and presentation may need attention first, but not every engagement includes all five stages.

Where recurring management is agreed, review relates to T: Track growth and improve performance. That stage depends on the project scope, baseline, timeframe and available client-authorised data. It is a method for reviewing evidence and making decisions, not a promise of growth.

A practical readiness review can lead to several reasonable decisions: prepare the campaign, clarify the listing first, revise the budget assumptions, or pause until the necessary information is available.
