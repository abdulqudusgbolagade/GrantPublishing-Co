# What a listing review can establish: My Dear Grandfather

A documented description, keyword and category project shows how to separate a practical listing diagnosis from claims the evidence cannot support.

## Start with the book, then examine its public signals

My Dear Grandfather is Barsha Rai’s novel, published by Cactus Rain Publishing. Its story is set in Nepal and involves Rick’s relationship with his ailing grandfather, family conflict, a murder and a missing family member. The listing project concerned description work, keyword research and category review. Cover design, advertising performance and a sales increase are not part of the documented claim.

The supplied earlier Kindle screenshot mixed story information and author biography in a dense description. That is a specific observation about presentation. It does not establish why a reader did or did not buy the book.

The distinction matters. A listing review can identify a confusing introduction or an inconsistent signal. It cannot infer conversion rates, private advertising results or a reader’s experience of the full manuscript from a public page alone.

## Give the description a clear job

For this novel, the description needed to help a reader understand the central situation and family tension before moving into information about the author. The project’s diagnosis was to bring that story forward, rather than make the introduction carry several unrelated jobs at once.

This is interpretation grounded in the supplied materials, not a measured conversion result. A useful description should represent the actual book. It should not add a genre promise, plot detail or claim the manuscript does not support.

Within the [GRANT Framework](https://grantpublishingco.com/about/#grant-framework), this connects to two stages: **R: Refine the positioning.** **A: Align the listing and presentation.** The documented work illustrates those connections. It does not establish completion of the full five-stage method.

## Treat keyword research as recommendations until implementation is evidenced

The report researched seven phrases, including “family secrets thriller”, “family murder mystery” and “missing person mystery book”. The case-study records do not show the final saved backend keyword fields or measured search volumes. We should therefore describe them as researched recommendations, not verified implemented fields or proven sources of traffic.

KDP’s official keyword guidance says authors can use up to seven keywords or short phrases. It recommends checking the relevance of candidate searches and avoiding misleading or prohibited terms. That is a platform guideline, not proof that a particular phrase will produce sales.

The practical question is whether a phrase accurately describes a reading experience the book can deliver. A phrase selected only because it appears popular can create the wrong expectation.

## Keep category recommendations separate from later visible placement

The research report proposed Amateur Sleuth, Domestic and Murder options. The later supplied paperback record showed Aging Parents (Books), Traditional Detective Mysteries (Books) and Family Saga Fiction. Those are different records.

We cannot use the later visible categories to prove that every researched option was selected, or infer the final Kindle category paths. A careful handover records what was recommended, what was implemented when evidence is available, and what remains unknown.

## A ranking snapshot is not a controlled result

The source includes a later paperback record of #2,936,816 in Books, #3,514 in Aging Parents, #16,588 in Traditional Detective Mysteries and #18,156 in Family Saga Fiction. The snapshot date was not supplied.

The earlier audit concerned Kindle and reported no visible Best Sellers Rank. The later record concerned paperback. Different editions and an undated later observation do not form a matched before-and-after comparison.

No verified sales increase or causal ranking improvement is established. An isolated ranking observation is also not evidence of ongoing growth tracking. Tracking would require an agreed scope, baseline, timeframe and suitable client-authorised data.

## A useful next step for your own listing

Write down one observation, one possible explanation and one missing piece of information. For example: “The opening description does not establish the central conflict” is an observation. “That may be making the book difficult to understand” is a hypothesis. The manuscript’s intended genre and relevant reader data may be missing.

Keeping those categories separate makes the next decision more specific.
